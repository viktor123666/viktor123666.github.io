The family name · the Nine Guardians

The Rönnkvist Council

Nine souls at the roots of the cork oak.
Without the Council, Ferdinand goes silent and the Seal fails.

RRunaÖÖgarNNivaNNjordKKvarVVidarIIskaSSmurjanTTova

Nine souls whose roles in the universe match the nine letters of the family name. They are guardians, watchers, keepers. They do not fight openly. They sit at the roots of Ferdinand the Cosmic Cork Oak and make sure his bark keeps regrowing: the bark the Architects cut for the Void-II Seal.

The Council meets once per cosmic year, on the night the moon stands directly under Ferdinand's lowest branch. Nine votes are cast, and the vote is always unanimous, because the Council never votes on outcomes. It votes on who speaks for the tree this year. It has been Smurjan three times. It has never been Kvar.

The Rönnkvist keep the tree alive.

Ferdinand

Runa The First Rune-Speaker

A Brygdar troll, nearly three metres tall when she stands, which she has not done in three hundred years; she sits bent on a stool of raw rowan. Grey-green stone skin mossed in the creases, a long lined face, long troll ears with seven notches along the rim, and translucent eyes the colour of fresh amber sap. Her pure-white braid runs down her back, across the floor and into the living wood, grown into the grain; silver runes are inlaid along both forearms and her palms are scarred black from carving. She wears a robe of cork shavings and rowan leaves layered like scales, and holds a black flint burin worn to the shape of her thumb.

VoiceDeep, quiet and grainy, in short troll sentences without contractions. When she speaks a rune, the world goes quiet to listen back.

The Spoken Rune Any of the twenty-four first runes she speaks aloud burns into reality as its meaning: Fire lights, Hold holds a falling ceiling, Way opens a path through solid wood, Rest puts an army to sleep. Her heartbeat technique, The Heartwood Beat, borrows one of Ferdinand's own heartbeats to speak a rune into every root in all nine realms at once.

Trollmage's great-great-grandmother, a Brygdar troll for whom Ferdinand once opened a door in his bark; she walked in, and has sat inside for three hundred and ten years. She lives in the Heartwood, the world inside the great tree: no one can climb Ferdinand, but a few are asked in. Her soul is the First Rune-Speaker, who in its first life carved twenty-four runes into the bark so the tree could be read, and has returned life after life since; her line in the oldest ledger has no rank field, because ranks were invented after her. Each rune she speaks burns fresh scars into her palms, and her braid has grown into the Heartwood, so if she cut it and walked out she would age three hundred years in a breath. Anyone who hears a first rune spoken learns it in one heartbeat, or goes deaf until spring.

Thanatos

Ögar The Far-Eye Watcher

Very tall and gaunt, standing like a heron in still water, with dark olive skin, very long fingers and dozens of thin ash-white braids to her hips, each tied with a tiny lead tag stamped with a name. A blindfold of black-green cypress thread covers her eyes; beneath it they are silver coins with no iris or pupil, and rose script circles the sockets and runs down her cheeks like old tear-tracks. She wears layered mourning linen in four shades of grey, a mantle of stripped cypress bark and soundless felt boots, with a pouch at her hip that clinks with silver tears.

VoiceSoft, certain and kind. She speaks of the future in the past tense, “You died well, there”, and reacts to everything a beat early.

The Lifted Blindfold Whoever she looks at sees their own death exactly as it will happen if nothing changes, and is given one heartbeat to choose differently. Her heartbeat technique, The Eight Thousand Blinks, looks down eight thousand roads inside a single beat to find the one blink in which a death can still be turned.

Once the finest Seer the Seers' Guild of Thanatos ever trained, until she learned who was buying the futures she saw; she bound away her sight of the present with a cypress blindfold and swore to look only to give a beat, never to sell one. She keeps watch at the Pillar of Names, a miles-high black column carved with every soul that has died, and moves through Thanatos with the blind lantern-children, always knowing where everyone will be. Every death she shows, she lives for one beat herself, and a silver coin rolls from each eye; the Guild has spent centuries stealing those tears to sell the future. Her visions are roads, not walls: the end of the road you are on, seen from far away. When she looks at Mingus she sees no ending at all, only a golden dog asleep in the sun, and she weeps for joy.

Kanten

Niva The Untraversed

Short, bow-legged and weathered, always leaning into a wind, with wind-cracked cheeks, a scar splitting her upper lip, two short black-and-grey braids and silver-coin eyes with no iris or pupil. She wears a bone-white reindeer-hide parka with bone toggles and a white-fox hood, fur-lined trousers and soft upturned boots, with a bone-handled knife, a coil of sinew rope and a rolled hide map at her belt. Across her back rides the seventh stake, a forearm-length spear of pale wood carved all over with tiny reindeer. An emerald ring of script circles her right wrist with seven notches, and six of them are filled in.

VoiceSparse. Three words where anyone else would use thirty.

The Stake She drives a stake into the Null and a hundred paces of real earth, grass and sky bloom around it for as long as it stands; within sight of one she can walk the void itself, ground appearing under each step. Her heartbeat technique, The Stake-Beat, plants the stake with exactly one heartbeat, and for that one beat the Null is counted.

A herder of Scalelist's far north who, seven thousand years ago, followed her herd into the Null, drove her tent-pole into nothing, and stood. Ground exists only where her stake is planted, and six planted stakes make Kanten, the Edge: six islands of real ground at the inner rim of the Null. The hard limit is seven stakes in seven thousand years, each carved from a branch Ferdinand drops once a millennium, and each costing a memory she chooses and carves onto the stake so she knows what she paid: where home was, her mother's voice, the colour of summer. She does not conquer the Null; she outlasts it, and her hide map is the only map of its edge. She tied a strip of her parka to Mingus's collar as a gift. “For winter.”

Heartbeat

Njord The Tide-Reader

A huge, barrel-chested fisherman in his sixties, slightly stooped, with forearms like mooring posts and hands bigger than his face. He is bald on top with grey-blonde hair tied back, a salt-bleached beard to his chest with one bone fish-hook bead, and pale grey-blue eyes whose irises move like water when he listens ahead. He wears an oilskin smock, sealskin boots and a sea-blue cloak worn grey at every edge, and a crimson net-pattern runs across his knuckles. He carries a driftwood tide-rod tied in seven knots, and his cloak always blows the wrong way, toward the weather that is coming.

VoiceSlow, low and salty, with pauses long enough to fish in. He calls everyone lad or lass, and often answers a question a minute before it is asked.

The Three-Day Ear He hears all weather three days early and ties unformed storms into the seven knots of his tide-rod, to be released later wherever he likes. His heartbeat technique, The Three-Day Swell, hears three days of sea inside a single beat and casts at the one swell that matters, so a whole storm turns on one flick of his wrist.

A fisherman of Tidekant, a village on the eastern shore of Heartbeat's great lake (old maps: Buddy's Lake) that appears on no Order map. He hears every wave three days before it forms, and can catch weather that hasn't happened yet in a knot. Knowing is not stopping: he heard the storm that drowned his two sons seven months out, and it came anyway. He is Kompis's distant cousin, though the two have never met, and he becomes the man who teaches Vigolas that lightning doesn't strike, it is answered. Every burn deafens him to the present; he is nearly deaf to voices now, and his granddaughter Sill is his ears.

Askslätten

Kvar The Last One

Average in every way: a tired, forgettable face that has been forty-five for a very long time, salt-and-pepper stubble, a burn scar along the left jaw, and short grey-brown hair with ash in it that never washes out. He wears a patched leather coat burned in a hundred places, every patch from a different war, and a scarf of dozens of regimental scarves knotted end to end in the colours of armies that no longer exist. A short spade, worn bright at the edge, rides on one shoulder. Amber script crawls across his back: the names of the dead he has buried.

VoicePlain, quiet and tired: the voice of a man who has said the same goodbyes a hundred times.

The Remainder Everything meant to end him bends away, and he outlasts every fight he walks into. His heartbeat technique, The Beat After, makes the last heartbeat on any field his own: however fast the others are, his single beat lands after their final one.

Kvar is always the one left standing at the end of any fight he is in. Blades, arrows and lightning route around him, but what misses him goes somewhere, so standing behind him in a battle is the most dangerous place in the world. He cannot decide a fight: the moment he raises a hand to win, the rule lets go of him, so he stands, and afterwards he buries. Whenever a war ends with no one left to remember it, Askslätten, the Ash Plain, surfaces out of the Current under its ashes, and he is on it with the ghost-soldiers who never left. The Order has tried to rank him nineteen times, and every time the ink fades by morning.

The Silence

Vidar The Silent Reckoner

A seated mountain in a robe, massive and sloping, two and a half metres tall on his stone bench, in heavy undyed grey felt layered like snowdrifts. His face is never seen: the deep hood is pure shadow, and it does not move when he breathes. Only his hands show, grey and cracked like dry clay, each the size of a plough blade, with violet script banded up the backs into the sleeves. Beside his bench, a ring of ten round hollows is carved into the rim-stone, and the filled ones glow violet.

VoiceHe never speaks aloud. He signs, fast, precise and full of terrible puns, and the Sign-speakers translate.

The Century-Sentence A full ring of ten silent centuries spent in one spoken sentence can unwrite even an Architect: chair, office and authority, gone from the world's record. It is also his heartbeat technique: a thousand years of unspoken words released inside one heartbeat.

Once the loudest of the Rönnkvist, a singer whose songs walked along the roots. A thousand years ago he climbed to the rim of Void-II, sat down at the edge of the Silence, and stopped making sound, saving his voice for the day it would be needed. For every hundred years of silence, one hollow in his ring fills with violet light, and when he finally speaks a single sentence aloud, every filled slot is spent at once and what the sentence names is rewritten. Any sound at all, a cough, a cry of pain, a laugh, would waste it, so he has learned to be hurt in silence and has never told anyone he loves them except with his hands. Bakasura has never tried to eat him.

Lumen

Iska The Spark-Carrier

A sturdy seven-year-old with scabbed knees, a round, gap-toothed, freckled face and copper-red braids tied with white ribbon that reach her knees; seven freckles across her nose glow emerald as she lights her lamps. Her hazel eyes are far older than her face. She wears a white linen shift, a soot-grey pinafore with seven pockets and a too-big white lamplighter's coat with the hem rolled up three times, and goes barefoot. She always holds up a lumpy clay lamp with a child's thumbprint pressed into its side, burning a pure white flame that gives no heat.

VoiceBright, direct and loud, with the cheer of a kid and the certainty of a mountain.

The Remembering Flame Anyone who looks into her lamp relives their happiest moment for one heartbeat, then chooses whether to fight harder or to stop. Her heartbeat technique, The Kindling Beat, lights the flame and the memory inside the same beat, so courage arrives before fear has time to.

Seven thousand years ago, when Lumen first went dark, a seven-year-old girl walked in out of the night with a clay lamp she had made herself and lit all seven of the island's towers. Lumen, an island of seven lamps, has not been fully dark since, and lost ships in the Current steer by its light. Her flame doesn't burn; it reminds. She has spent every day she would have grown on it, so she will never be eight, and the flame is made of her own happiest moment, burning a little shorter each time she shows someone theirs. The Dimmers, grey-masked thieves who bottle stolen joy and sell it, want Lumen put out.

Trinity

Smurjan The Trinity

Tall, gaunt and very still, slate-grey skin, a long white braid, and three eyes — the third, in the centre of his forehead, rimmed with crimson when it opens. Faint crimson script-scars cover his chest and arms like writing under the skin. A torn tri-coloured robe in void-black, hunger-red and ash-grey. Barefoot. Wherever he stands, the light throws three shadows that do not match him.

VoiceQuiet and measured; he calls the body “we”. When the others cut in, their voices come out of his mouth — Kali sharp and fast, Bakasura slow and deep — and under it all murmur the voices of everyone the Trinity has carried.

The Sightline A crimson line drawn from his third eye to the one exact point of a thing, which he walks along step by perfect step and strikes with an open palm.

One body, three souls: Smurjan (Wrath, the host), Kali (who borrows another fighter's sin for sixty seconds) and Bakasura (a colossal maw that eats what it bites). Whatever dies in Trinity comes back slightly altered, and so does he — every respawn changes him a little. He met the crew as an enemy and joined them on his knees. In the HADOM Arena he defeated Vigolas, and the captain's lightning opened his Wrath Codex. He wants to free the voices inside him, and to find out who split him.

Vägskälet

Tova The Thunder-Herald

A long-distance runner, lean and long-legged, with a sunburned nose, dust in her laugh lines, one eyebrow usually raised, dawn-gold eyes and a raven-black braid tied off with red signal-cloth. She wears a long dust-brown road-coat split at the back for running, its pockets stuffed with other people's letters, over a leather harness, wrapped shins and dusty boots. Across her back rides the horn: a great curved horn of pale, cork-ridged bark, longer than her arm, like a crescent moon over one shoulder. When she sounds it, gold script blazes from the corners of her mouth down her throat.

VoiceDry, wry and clear, and mostly addressed to the horn. When the horn speaks through her, the news comes in huge capitals that run off every edge of the page.

The True Horn When she blows it, every road and every realm hears the same true news in the same instant. Her heartbeat technique, All Roads at Once, sends one sounding down all twelve great roads of the Crossroads inside a single beat: nine to the nine realms, one to the Mouth of the Current, one to the Deep, and one overgrown road nobody walks.

A road-courier's daughter from Vägskälet, the crossroads island where every gate in every realm meets. At twelve she found the horn lying at the exact centre of the junction, where no road goes, and it trembled in her hands; she has been the Herald ever since. The horn cannot sound a lie, and it trembles only for news that changes the world, such as the Three Signs or the fall of something great. She cannot refuse a true sounding, however much it hurts to say, and each one leaves her too breathless to run for a day. In chapter 27 she blew the First Sign: AN ARCHITECT HAS FALLEN.
