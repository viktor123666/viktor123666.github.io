The Ferdinand Scarwood

VIKTOR were chosen. RÖNNKVIST were appointed.
FERDINAND were never asked. They grew.

Ferdinand is a cork oak. Cork oaks get stripped and live anyway. The bark grows back, and the Rönnkvist Council sits at the roots to make sure it does.

But a tree remembers every wound. Hit a cork oak hard enough, often enough, and it doesn't just heal over. It grows a burl, a knotted, swollen lump of the hardest wood on the tree. Ferdinand has nine of them, one where each of his roots enters each of the Nine Realms, and over enough ages each burl hardened into a soul.

They aren't blood. They don't count toward the fifteen, and they will never sit a family seat. They are wood: Ferdinand's own anger, given legs.

The Rönnkvist keep the tree alive. The Scarwood keep it from forgiving.

F FERDINAND-1

root · Scalelist

Flisa The Splinter

A child-height girl, 1.10 m and still shrinking, built from broken cork-oak shards fitted together like a jagged puzzle, with amber sap running from the cracks like blood. Her face is splintered wood with a mouth full of splinter-teeth, and her eyes are two knot-holes with a sap-gold glint under wooden lids that have not blinked in a thousand years. A crown of curling cork shavings stands up like thorns, her bark ridges grow in the shape of a ragged dress, and she wears a necklace of iron nails pulled out of Ferdinand's roots. A burl over her heart glows sap-gold when she uses her power.

Splinter-Seed Anything she touches keeps a splinter of her, and from anywhere in the universe she can step into any splinter she has left and climb out of it, even out of the hilt of your own sword. Her heartbeat technique, Splinter-Pulse, splits one heartbeat into every splinter at once, so a thousand doors swell shut and a thousand palms sting in the same beat.

The first of the Ferdinand Scarwood: the tree's anger, grown where the first strip of bark was ever torn from him at his Scalelist root. She is wood, not a soul the Order ever assigned, so she has no sin and no rank, only a burl. For a thousand years she has seeded herself into the prime world so that no one could hurt the tree there without her knowing, and every root of the Splinterlands holds a splinter of her. Every splinter is a piece of her, so the more places she can be, the less of her is anywhere: she was once a soldier's height and now stands at a child's. Iron and salt-fire kill her splinters, and she hates the Barkstrippers who harvest Ferdinand, and anyone who sails on stolen bark.

E FERDINAND-2

root · Codexia

Ekhard The Unfelled

A colossus of grey ridged bark, six metres tall kneeling, sunk to his knees in the stone floor of the Ring Halls beneath Codexia, where he has not moved in three hundred years. His face has a split for a mouth, a moss beard and brows like shelf fungus, and two old axe-wounds have healed into eye-shapes with a grey-green light inside; ferns grow on his head, and the Library mice have worn paths through them. Nine axes, a sword, a spear and the tooth of something that bit him once stand out of his back like a fence. Across his chest a sheared cross-section shows thousands of faintly glowing growth rings, and the spent ones are black.

Ringbreak Whenever he chooses, he cracks a ring and releases its stored blow, aimed wherever he points or back at whoever struck it, wherever they are now. His heartbeat technique, The Ring-Crack, releases any number of rings inside one heartbeat: three thousand years of violence in a single beat.

The second of the Ferdinand Scarwood, grown where the tree's root enters Codexia, on the spot where the Order's first assault on the Library struck. Nothing can cut, break or fell him: every blow ever struck at him becomes a growth ring that keeps the blow's full force, and Codexia keeps a shelf of nothing but tracings of those rings, the most complete history of violence in the universe. The rings are his memory, and breaking one erases the year it was made; he has forgotten his own name twice, and the Library mice keep it written on a scrap of paper. He has never struck first. Kneeling across the corridor, he says “Hit me”, and the only way to earn passage is to refuse.

R FERDINAND-3

root · Trinity

Röta The Rot-Mother

Tall, slow and slightly bowed, like a tree in fog, with a long neck and long arms. A ledge of shelf-fungus half-veils her kind face, her pale eyes are ridged like mushroom gills and glow a soft yellow-green, and mushrooms grow from her collarbones. Her hair is a mass of black leaves and moss where beetles nest and moths drift out when she moves. She wears a robe of matted black leaves whose hem crawls with things being born, and she smells like a forest floor after a month of rain; nobody gets used to it.

Compost She rots anything at all, and from the rot something new grows that she chooses: rot a tyrant's crown and an orchard comes up. Her heartbeat technique, The Long Rot-Beat, is a single heartbeat a minute long, in which everything in her reach decays a whole season before her choice bursts into green.

The third of the Ferdinand Scarwood, grown where the tree's root enters Trinity, the realm where whatever dies comes back slightly altered. She is the soil between the death and the respawn: the Compost Fields, where every Trinity soul has lain at least once. She can rot anything, steel or stone or an oath or a rank, and from the rot grows whatever she chooses. One of her heartbeats lasts a full minute and nothing living can stand near her for longer, so the mother of a thousand orchards, forests and mushroom folk has never been allowed to hold a single thing. Salt is the one thing she cannot rot, and she is sad and patient, never cruel.

D FERDINAND-4

root · The Cut

Dolk The Knife-Child

A thin child-shaped thing, 1.25 m of ribs and elbows, with grey bark-skin smooth as peeled wood, huge dark eyes rimmed in indigo, and black hair he hacks short himself. From the elbow down his right forearm is a long blade of black obsidian with an indigo edge, which he holds carefully away from his body and from everyone. He is wrapped like a mummy in grey-brown scab-strips, each one a bond, a contract or a name he has cut. Only the wound is left bare: a long cut across his chest, shoulder to hip, that never closes, never bleeds, and glows faintly indigo inside.

The First Cut One silent stroke severs what has no body: an alliance, a contract, a rank, a name off a face. His heartbeat technique, The Skipped Beat, cuts the gap after one of your heartbeats so that the next one never comes, and you stagger a beat late; he acts between beats, never in them.

The fourth of the Ferdinand Scarwood, and the saddest. When the Architects cut Ferdinand's bark to make the Void-II Seal, he was the cut; the wound hardened into a burl, and the burl into a child. The wound across his chest is that first cut, still open, and as long as it stays open the Seal holds, so he stands guard at the Cut alone, seen only from outside. He must carry everything he severs, and he cannot hold a hand, a cup or a gift without cutting it. There are five things he cannot cut, the same five the Null cannot uncount: a heartbeat, the HADOM Quoka, love, Mingus, and the Count.

I FERDINAND-5

root · Thanatos

Igel The Leech

Long, low and boneless, 2.3 m stretched out but always hunched and flowing like an eel standing up. His face is a smooth grey oval with no eyes, a small round mouth and only a ridge where a nose should be; when he speaks in a dead person's voice, their face flickers across his like a reflection on moving water. His wet grey skin never dries, and in his palms are round mouths ringed with tiny teeth that open and close by themselves. He wears only a sash of drowned rope knotted with small things the dead left in the river: a ring, a key, a child's tooth.

Borrowed Pulse His palm-mouths latch onto anything that counts and drink its tempo, so he moves at whatever speed he drank, from slow as stone to nearly nine hundred actions a heartbeat. His heartbeat technique, The Draught, drinks one of your heartbeats inside that same beat: your tempo drops by exactly as much as his rises.

The fifth of the Ferdinand Scarwood, grown where the tree's root enters Thanatos, on the bed of the slow river where the gentle dead drift between lives. He was born hungry and without a heart, and lives on leftovers: the last few beats the dead no longer need, by the one rule Fenna, the Witness, set for him. While a dead person's pulse is in him he speaks with their voice and remembers what they remembered, and he carries last words home to families for free. A day without drinking and he stops, still as river-stone. He cannot drink Mingus, which is like trying to drink the river itself, or the Null, which is zero.

N FERDINAND-6

root · Astralis

Nässla The Nettle

Lean, wiry and feral, 1.78 m of tendon, holding her arms slightly out from her sides so they brush nothing. Her green-black skin is covered head to toe in fine stinging hairs that catch the light like frost, with red welts on everything she has touched, including her own cheeks. Her hair is a thicket of tall serrated nettle stems that flower in tiny green tassels in summer. She wears a scratchy grey-green shift woven from her own nettle fibre and a belt of braided stems, and her hands are always bare: she has never worn gloves and never will.

Sting-Truth Anyone she stings cannot lie while it burns, and every lie they try burns hotter and pushes a nettle out through their skin. Her heartbeat technique, The Sting-Count, adds one heartbeat to her own for every lie spoken within her reach, so in a room full of liars she becomes the fastest thing alive.

The sixth of the Ferdinand Scarwood, grown where the tree's root enters Astralis, on the spot where the first promise ever made to the tree was broken. She walks the Nettle Field between the Mirrors, Astralis's two great sealing Mirrors that hold Pride and Envy themselves, so the sealed sins never find a lie to slip out through. She cannot pretend to be anything she isn't, so mirrors do not reflect her and reflection-powers cannot touch her. Her nettles grow wherever a promise was ever broken, which lets her track every broken promise in the universe. She cannot touch anything without hurting it, has never been held, and cannot even tell a kind lie, not even to herself.

A FERDINAND-7

root · The Burn

Aska The Ash-Born

Slight and straight-backed, still as a candle flame in a windless room. Her charcoal-black skin is cracked all over, the cracks glowing like embers, and the inside of her mouth glows like a hearth when she speaks. Her eyes are two live coals and her long hair is smoke, drifting upward and smelling of a house fire. She wears a dress of fine grey ash that trails like smoke when she moves, walks barefoot, and leaves black footprints that are still warm a day later.

One Minute She relights any ash into what it was for exactly sixty seconds: a burned house stands, a burned letter reads itself aloud, a burned person opens their eyes and says one last thing. Her heartbeat technique, Sixty, lends the burned their own last sixty heartbeats back, counted aloud, and on the sixtieth it ends.

The seventh of the Ferdinand Scarwood, born when the sun of Helios first struck Ferdinand's highest branch; in Helios commands arrive before their causes, so her fire existed before the spark did. She lives on the Burn, the scorched tip of that branch where it dips into the Current at the edge of Helios's sky: no one can climb to it, but you can sail there. For thousands of years the bereaved have come by boat with jars of ash, and she gives each of them one minute. She burns again with every minute she grants, her cracks widening a hair each time, and she still says yes every time she is asked; nobody has ever given her a minute. Ash from salt-fire will never relight.

N FERDINAND-8

root · Heartbeat

Nagel The Nail

At rest, a single rusted, square-headed iron nail driven into the lintel above the cabin door. Awake, a tall, gaunt man of rusted iron, as if hammered out of one long bar, the top of his head hammered flat and crowded with the dents of a thousand strikes. His eyes are two rivet-holes glowing with the cabin's reflected firelight, and snow from outside the cabin collects on his head and shoulders. He bleeds rust, creaks at every joint, and wherever he stands the wood beneath him blackens in a ring.

Pin He becomes a nail mid-stride and drives himself through anything, pinning a shadow to a wall, a person to a moment or a memory into a mind, and nothing he pins can move, flee, fall or be forgotten. His heartbeat technique, The Pinned Beat, makes one heartbeat of one person come again and again, the way the cabin's moment does.

The eighth of the Ferdinand Scarwood, and the only one made of iron. One of Ferdinand's roots runs under the cabin in Heartbeat; the night the cabin was first lit, someone drove a nail into the lintel to hang a lantern on, the iron poisoned the root beneath, and the root, instead of dying, grew the nail a soul. Every heartbeat the cabin resets, the fire, the snow, Kompis and the golden dog at the hearth, and Nagel is the one thing that doesn't, so he remembers every beat. He is the cabin's witness and its guard, and he has never come inside and never will. Every beat, Mingus sniffs at the door where he is: a billion sniffs, and his only friend.

D FERDINAND-9

root · The Null

Draug The Undrowned

A bloated, 2.2 m hulk of waterlogged deadwood in the shape of a man, swollen wide at chest and belly, grey-green and crusted with barnacles. Black weed hangs over his face like hair, his eye-pits glow a dim sea-green under barnacle crusts, and water pours from his hanging mouth when he talks. Strands of old net, a rope-end and a rusted ship's chain have grown into him. His right fist is always closed against his chest, and he leaves puddles that never dry.

Sunken Return He wades into the Null's edge and drags half-taken things back before the Null finishes them: half a city, half a name, a fading soul. His heartbeat technique, The Count-Back, starts his heart at zero as he climbs out and counts it back up, and every beat he counts makes what he carries one beat more counted.

The ninth of the Ferdinand Scarwood, grown on the root Ferdinand sent into the Null, the root that drowned. He lives at the Tideline, the Null's drowning edge, where things are halfway through being uncounted. When he first went under he was holding something he loved, and because the Null cannot uncount love, he came back; he does not remember what it was. Niva, who walked the Null and mapped it, found him at the edge and never put him on the map. He cannot die, since killed he sinks and climbs out again, but everything he drags back comes back slightly wrong, and so does he: each return, more water, less of him.

The Law of the Wood

Not blood, but wood.The fifteen souls of the Lineage and the Author as the sixteenth stand exactly as canon has them. The Scarwood is a third order, of the tree and not of the family.
Loyal to one thing.They don't care about the five fragments, the Architects or the Arena. They care about the tree. Whoever threatens Ferdinand meets the Scarwood.
The five uncuttable.A heartbeat, the HADOM Quoka, love, Mingus and the Count. What Dolk cannot cut is what the Null cannot uncount.
