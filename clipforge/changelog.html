Changelog

What changed, and what it measured

Every entry carries the before-and-after number. Where something is still broken, it says so.

9 August 2026 · Detection

Kills are now read from the picture

The engine reads both teams' kill counters from the scoreboard — a tiny strip of pixels, OCR only when they change, a value accepted only after two identical consecutive reads. A silent multi-kill nobody reacted to out loud now scores like the fight it was. Match resets are recognised, other games without a scoreboard are detected and skipped, and the signal is additive — when it is absent, selection behaves exactly as before. Timing granularity is ~2 seconds (keyframe sampling); confirmed kills land in selection with an 8-second reach.

silent multi-kills on an 82-min stream 0 → 3 bursts caught (+4, +3, +5)
Known limitation · open

Speaker colours stop at cross-talk

When two people talk simultaneously the voice embedding blends and matches neither cleanly. We colour fewer lines rather than colour them wrongly. More voice profiles raise the ceiling; nothing else we have tried does. The enrolment pipeline is built and waiting — this closes the day the remaining voice recordings are enrolled.

coloured lines ~9 % of a busy stream
30 July 2026 · accuracy

Cohort normalisation for speaker matching

With a single voice profile there was nothing to compare against, so the threshold alone decided — and it failed in both directions at once, missing the right person and occasionally colouring the wrong one. Now the engine builds a cohort from the other voices in the same stream and asks whether a line resembles you more than anyone else present.

separation 0.341 → 0.559 recall 95 % → 100 %
30 July 2026 · safety

Moments, not just words

Masking a slur does not save a clip that is about the joke. A separate gate now removes such passages from selection entirely, before any clip is built. It matches word stems rather than whole words, after a truncated slur slipped past a list that required full endings.

reference stream 5 of 194 segments blocked
29 July 2026 · quality

Highlight detection rebuilt around measurement

Laughter is now found in the audio's amplitude modulation rather than by hoping the transcript spells it out — and gated, because rhythmic speech modulates identically and was ranking highest. Hype is counted as density over a rolling window instead of per line, since a single exclamation carries no signal.

dull callouts score 0.00 clip length spread 5.6 s → 11.5 s
29 July 2026 · transcription

Invented lines removed

On silent passages the transcriber was repeating its own instructions back as if someone had said them — over two hundred fabricated lines reached the screen in one stream. Output is now compared against the instruction and matches are discarded, and the instructions themselves are written in a register nobody speaks aloud.

invented lines 202 → 0 high-confidence 81 % → 95 %
28 July 2026 · transcription

Game-aware vocabulary

The transcriber was being fed one game's terminology while a different game was on screen. Vocabulary is a bias, not a hint — the wrong one produces confident nonsense rather than degrading gracefully. The game is now detected first, from three short unbiased samples.

detection ~35 s lines below floor 21.1 % → 7.6 %
28 July 2026 · infrastructure

Rendering moved off the operator's machine

Audio cleaning, speaker matching and all rendering now run on our own servers. The one stage that still needs a graphics card is transcription, which is rented by the minute on demand.

local CPU load 0 % speaker matching 8 min 35 s → 6 min, remote
28 July 2026 · compatibility

Any source profile, not just one

The pipeline assumed 1080p60 at 48 kHz and silently produced nothing on a 720p 30 fps recording. Resolution, frame rate and audio are now read from the file. Thresholds that describe areas scale quadratically rather than linearly.

subtitle pixel check 0 % → 100 %
17 July 2026 · first delivery

First full stream through the pipeline

End to end: transcription, speaker separation, cutting, captions, audio mastering, thumbnail and shorts — from a raw two-and-a-half-hour recording with no manual editing.
